// SCOPE DECISION

Two systems sharing a screen, not one system

The build is a hybrid, but the two halves have different epistemic status and must be kept apart in code and in the writeup. The disruption engine is the priority and carries all ML validity; the vessel-safety layer is a live monitoring demo that sits on top. Conflating them would reintroduce the exact "dashboard over substance" failure mode this project exists to avoid.

PRIORITY · BACKTESTED
Disruption early-warning engine
Answers "is Hormuz about to seize up — should a shipper reroute?" Port-level and macro. Three DL arms → gradient-boosting fusion → chokepoint risk index → LLM agentic brief via NVIDIA NIM. Validated against historical events with strict temporal splits. This is what the Day 5 gate tests.
LAYER · LIVE DEMO
Per-vessel safety alerting
Answers "what is this ship doing right now?" Live AIS geofenced to the strait + UAE approaches. Rule-based, not ML: risk-zone entry, transponder-dark events, anomalous loitering. Not backtestable on a one-week timeline — scoped explicitly as real-time monitoring, never as a validated forecast.
The one clean bridge: the disruption risk index modulates the vessel-alert threshold — high macro risk tightens per-vessel sensitivity. That is the only coupling allowed between the two systems. Everything else stays walled off.
// WORKFLOW

How data becomes a warning — end to end

The pipeline moves left to right: raw sources are ingested and normalised, each modality is scored by its own model, the three scores fuse into one index, and the index drives both the dashboard and the agentic brief. The vessel-safety layer runs in parallel off live AIS and only reads the risk index to set its alert sensitivity.

01
Ingest & normalise SOURCES
Scheduled pulls from Copernicus (SAR + optical tiles over the four ports), EIA (Brent + tanker series), GDELT/ACLED (geofenced Gulf events), and a live aisstream socket. Each source lands in a raw store, then a normaliser aligns timestamps and geographies onto a common grid.
02
Score per modality 3 ARMS
CNN turns satellite tiles into a per-port congestion index. VAE/TFT turns market series into an anomaly score. The transformer turns event text into a geopolitical tension score. Each arm runs as its own containerised service so they scale and fail independently.
03
Fuse into one index GBM
A gradient-boosting model combines the three arm outputs into a single chokepoint risk index (0–100), trained only on past data via TimeSeriesSplit. The CI leakage test fails the build if any future information reaches the fusion layer.
04
Serve, brief & alert OUTPUT
The index and per-arm breakdown stream to the dashboard. An LLM agent (NVIDIA NIM) writes a plain-language brief explaining why the index moved. In parallel, the vessel layer raises rule-based AIS alerts, with its threshold tightened when the index is high.

// DELIVERABLES

What the dashboard actually shows

The dashboard has one job: turn the fused signal into decisions a shipping or policy team can act on in seconds. Six focused surfaces, nothing decorative.

🎯Chokepoint risk index
The headline number, 0–100, updated as new data lands, with a 30-day trend and the threshold bands (watch / elevated / critical) marked.
🧩Per-arm contribution
Which arm is driving the index right now — congestion, market, or geopolitics — so a user can see whether the signal is physical, financial, or political.
🗺️Live vessel map
Real-time AIS over the strait and UAE approaches, with risk-zone rings and colour-coded vessels (normal, warning, transponder-dark).
⚓Port congestion panel
Per-port berth occupancy and anchorage counts for Jebel Ali, Khalifa, Fujairah, Khor Fakkan — Fujairah highlighted as the outside-the-strait tell.
🚨Alert feed
Rolling log of triggered events — transponder-dark, loitering, risk-zone entry, index threshold crossings — each timestamped and routable.
📝Agentic brief
A plain-language paragraph, generated per update, that explains why the index moved and names the recommended posture. Not just a number — a reason.
// INSIGHTS

The questions it answers

Every surface exists to answer a concrete operational question. These are the ones the dashboard is built to resolve at a glance.

RISK NOW
Is Hormuz stable, watch, or critical today — and how fast is that changing?
WHY
Is the signal driven by ships piling up, oil markets pricing fear, or escalating rhetoric — and which came first?
REROUTE?
Should cargo divert now, and is Fujairah (outside the strait) already absorbing rerouted traffic — an early physical confirmation?
WHICH SHIPS
Which specific vessels are in a risk zone, loitering, or have gone dark — the ones a duty officer should watch right now?
LEAD TIME
How far ahead of the last comparable event did the index rise — the honest measure of how much warning it buys?

// STAKEHOLDER VALUE

Why it matters to UAE companies & government

The UAE sits directly on the world's most critical oil chokepoint. Roughly a fifth of global oil moves past its coastline, and Fujairah is one of the planet's largest bunkering hubs. Early warning here is not academic — it is operational and fiscal.

GOVERNMENT / REGULATOR
Situational awareness & contingency lead time
A single fused picture across imagery, markets, and events gives maritime and energy authorities earlier, evidence-backed warning to pre-position resources, coordinate with UKMTO, and brief decision-makers — instead of reacting to headlines. The agentic brief turns model output into something a policy team can circulate immediately.
SHIPPING / ENERGY / INSURANCE
Reroute, hedge, and price risk on time
For carriers and traders, hours of lead time on a Hormuz disruption is money: reroute decisions, war-risk premium timing, and fuel hedging all hinge on it. Fujairah congestion as an early physical confirmation lets logistics teams act before rates spike. Insurers get an independent signal to price Gulf transit risk.
PORTS / TERMINALS
Anticipate surge and diverted traffic
When traffic reroutes around the strait, UAE ports outside it absorb the surge. Advance signal helps terminals plan berth allocation and anchorage capacity before vessels arrive, rather than after.
STRATEGIC
Sovereign-relevant, built on open data
Because it runs on public satellite, market, and event data, the system is independent of any single commercial feed and can be operated in-country — a meaningful property for infrastructure this close to national energy security.

// HONEST LIMITATIONS

Named failure modes with mitigations

CRITICAL
Label scarcity — ~5 real disruption events
A handful of true events (2012, 2019 ×2, 2024–25, 2026 window) cannot support conventional supervised training. The engine is an anomaly/early-warning system, not a classifier with statistically robust event odds.
→ Frame as anomaly detection; report per-event behaviour, not a headline accuracy number.
CRITICAL
Backtest leakage risk
With so few events, it is trivially easy to let future information bleed into training and get a flattering backtest that means nothing.
→ TimeSeriesSplit everywhere; CI leakage test fails the build; synthetic data tagged and barred from the gate.
HIGH
Vessel layer is not a predictor
The per-vessel safety alerts are live rule-based monitoring on real-time AIS. They are not backtested and make no forecast claim.
→ Scoped and labelled as a monitoring demo throughout; walled off from the ML validity of the engine.
HIGH
Container OOM on rasters + GDELT noise
Full Sentinel scenes blow container memory; GDELT is high-volume and noisy, with false geopolitical signal.
→ Tile and stream rasters; de-noise GDELT against ACLED; concept-drift monitoring on all arms.
