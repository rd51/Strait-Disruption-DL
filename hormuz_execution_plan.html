// SCOPE DECISION

Two systems sharing a screen, not one system

The build is a hybrid, but the two halves have different epistemic status and are kept strictly apart in code. The disruption engine carries all ML validity and is the project's core contribution. The vessel-safety layer is an honest monitoring demo that sits on top — and its data-source reality has now been measured, not assumed.

PRIORITY · BACKTESTED · ✅ BUILT & MEASURED
Disruption early-warning engine
Answers "is Hormuz about to seize up?" Port-level and macro. Three DL arms → weighted rule fusion → chokepoint risk index → Claude-generated analyst brief. Validated with causal leave-one-event-out backtest on real disruption events. 2 of 3 scoreable events detected, median 36-day lead.
LAYER · LIVE MONITORING · ⚠️ DATA-SOURCE CONSTRAINED
Per-vessel safety alerting
Answers "what is this ship doing right now?" Rule-based, not ML: risk-zone entry, transponder-dark events, anomalous loitering. aisstream.io has zero AIS receiver coverage in the Gulf — measured, not assumed (0 messages in 60s vs 101 msg/s in Europe on the same key). The vessel layer uses Global Fishing Watch as a 4-day-lag backtest source. The /vessels endpoint returns 501 with a full explanation.
The one clean bridge: the disruption risk index modulates the vessel-alert threshold — high macro risk tightens per-vessel sensitivity. That is the only coupling allowed between the two systems. Everything else stays walled off. Satellite (Arm A) detects vessels independently of AIS transponders, which is exactly why that arm exists — and why the AIS coverage gap strengthens rather than breaks the architecture.
// WORKFLOW

From raw sources to a warning — end to end, as executed

The pipeline moves left to right: raw sources are ingested and normalised, each modality is scored by its own model, the three scores fuse into one index, and the index drives the dashboard and agentic brief. All four stages are built. The vessel safety layer runs in parallel off the risk index only — its own source is constrained.

01
Ingest & normalise SOURCES ✅ DONE
GDELT 2.0 Translingual: live 15-min poller (Dockerised, SIGTERM-aware, idempotent restarts) + full historical backfill. Three anchor windows downloaded: 934,948 rows total. Three-way Gulf filter (bbox + FIPS country + keyword) confirmed load-bearing — keyword leg uniquely caught Houthi strikes on Saudi tankers that pure geofencing dropped.

Market (Arm B): 2,204 days × 11 series, 2018–2026. FRED official (Brent, WTI, natural gas) + yfinance supplement (BZ=F, CL=F, tanker ETFs). Weekends left as NaN — filling is a modelling decision, not an ingest decision.

Sentinel-1 SAR (Arm A): 449 chips at 1024 px across 4 ports × 4 anchor windows, 5,947 processing units (20% of monthly quota). Orbit direction recorded on every chip (223 ascending / 219 descending). Coverage class written as a JSON sidecar — partial chips never compared against full ones.

AIS: aisstream.io returns 0 messages in the Gulf (measured). Code is valid — same code pulls 1023 messages in 50s from the English Channel. Source has no Gulf coverage; see scope note.
02
Score per modality 3 ARMS ✅ BUILT
Arm A (SAR): CA-CFAR with dual-pol agreement (VV + VH must agree — suppresses sea clutter) and a water mask validated by stable land-detection counts (cranes and quays are constant across all windows). CNN (MobileNetV2 transfer) trained on 12,687 patches. CNN test accuracy 0.837 — loses to a random forest on brightness statistics (0.946). At 15.6 m/px a vessel is 1–3 pixels; there is no spatial structure for convolution to exploit. CFAR remains the baseline. Arm A assigned weight 0.00 in fusion.

Arm B (Market VAE): LSTM encoder → 8-dimensional latent → LSTM decoder. 20-trading-day windows. 9 stationary market features including spread_days_gt15_30d (the Brent–WTI spread's cleanest Gulf-specific discriminator). Trained on non-event windows only. AUC 0.680. Detects 1 of 5 events (2026 Hormuz, 38-day lead). β warm-up annealing (0→0.1 over first third) prevented posterior collapse — β=1.0 from step 0 produced KL 0.134, near total collapse.

Arm C (Text): Zero-shot contrastive scoring — cosine similarity to 10 disruption anchor sentences MINUS similarity to 6 calm anchor sentences, applied to URL slugs extracted from GDELT SOURCEURL fields. 228,299 of 424,473 archived URLs (53.8%) yield 4+ alphabetic words — headlines already on disk, no API needed. AUC 0.848. Detects 3 of 3 sharp incidents. Best-performing arm, best generalisation.
03
Fuse into one index WEIGHTED RULE ⚠️ GBM DEGENERATE
With ~5 labels the GBM fusion is statistically degenerate: labelling a 30-day pre-onset window produces 233 of 282 rows positive (83%) — two of three folds are single-class. The weighted rule (Arm B 0.50 · Arm C 0.50 · Arm A 0.00) is not a workaround; it is the honest design given label scarcity. Scores are converted to causal expanding percentiles (ranked against past data only) before combining. Leakage gate: no derived feature may outlive or out-count its source. TimeSeriesSplit + 10-day embargo on all feature construction.
04
Serve, brief & alert FastAPI · DuckDB ✅ LIVE
24 FastAPI endpoints serving arm scores, risk index, SAR chip rendering, port geography, freshness status, live model inference, and the semantic registry. DuckDB queries hive-partitioned parquet directly — no separate database process; the raw store is the truth. Semantic registry: 22 metrics, 25 prohibitions (the forbidden field is injected into the Claude brief system prompt). /vessels returns 501 with an honest explanation rather than an empty map. Claude brief layer: every number computed before the API call; Claude explains and prioritises, never recalls. Prohibitions from the registry guard against the most common misstatements. Kafka layer: built, profiled, and honestly labelled — 16,557× slower than direct path at 1.0 row/sec actual throughput vs 1,000,000 msg/s Kafka design point.

The coastline split is the thesis

20 million barrels per day transit the Strait of Hormuz. 11 of 13 UAE ports sit inside the strait — they live or die with its navigability. The other two — Fujairah and Khor Fakkan — are on the Gulf of Oman coast, outside the strait entirely. When Hormuz is stressed, diverted traffic reroutes to these two.

Fujairah is the world's second-largest bunkering hub. Congestion spiking there is the earliest physical confirmation of disruption — ships rerouting before any official announcement. The model watches Fujairah before any other port. The viewer makes this split immediately visible.

What the measured data showed: Fujairah vessel counts were +0.0% across the 2026 onset. The thesis is geographically correct; the CFAR signal was not sensitive enough to detect it. The viewer still matters — it teaches the geography the model must learn.

// OPERATIONAL QUESTIONS

What the system answers — and what it honestly cannot

RISK NOW
Is Hormuz stable, watch, or critical today? The risk index is updated whenever new data lands, with a 30-day trend and threshold bands visible.
WHY
Is the signal driven by market anomaly (Arm B) or geopolitical text (Arm C)? Arm A contributes zero — that is a finding, not a gap. Per-arm breakdown is always shown alongside the index.
REROUTE?
The system flags Fujairah congestion as the early physical confirmation. Measured result: Fujairah counts did not move in 2026. The satellite signal was not sensitive enough at 15.6 m/px. A larger chip (1600px) may show it — not yet tested.
LEAD TIME
43 days on 2026 Hormuz. 30 days on Abqaiq. Caveats: 2 of 3 scoreable events, ~5 total labels. Lead time is per-event, not an average across a statistically robust sample. State this whenever the number is quoted.
WHICH SHIPS
Not available for Gulf waters. aisstream has no Gulf receiver coverage (measured). The dashboard architecture supports it; the source does not. Global Fishing Watch as satellite AIS is the next candidate — 4-day lag.

// STAKEHOLDER VALUE

Why it matters to UAE companies & government

The UAE sits directly on the world's most critical oil chokepoint. 2026 provided a live test — and the system fired 43 days before the measurable onset on the first real Hormuz disruption in decades. Fujairah is the planet's second-largest bunkering hub. Early warning here is operational and fiscal, not academic.

GOVERNMENT / REGULATOR
43 days of situational lead time on a real event
The 2026 Hormuz crisis was detected 43 days before the price-confirmed onset — while the system was in development, running on live data. Maritime and energy authorities with access to this signal could have pre-positioned resources and coordinated before the June headlines. The Claude brief turns model output into something a policy team can circulate immediately, with prohibitions built in against the most misleading framings.
SHIPPING / ENERGY / INSURANCE
Reroute, hedge, and price risk on time
For carriers and traders, 43 days of lead time on a Hormuz closure is money: reroute decisions, war-risk premium timing, and fuel hedging all hinge on early detection. The Brent–WTI spread (WTI is landlocked; widening prices chokepoint risk specifically) is the cleanest single discriminator — verified across 2018–2026 against COVID, Ukraine, Red Sea, and the 2026 crisis.
PORTS / TERMINALS
Anticipate surge and diverted traffic
When traffic reroutes around the strait, UAE bypass ports absorb the surge. The system monitors Fujairah and Khor Fakkan specifically for this signal. The 2026 reroute was not visible in CFAR counts at current resolution — this is an honest finding that shapes the next hardware investment, not a failure to hide.
STRATEGIC
Sovereign-relevant, built on open data
Runs on Copernicus (EU public satellite), FRED/EIA (US federal), and GDELT (open). No commercial data feeds in the backtest loop. Can be operated in-country, audited, and extended. The semantic registry's prohibition layer ensures model outputs carry their own epistemic caveats — the system cannot be misquoted by the brief it generates.

// HONEST LIMITATIONS

What this system cannot do — measured, not assumed

CRITICAL
Label scarcity — ~5 real disruption events
Cannot support conventional supervised training. The system is an anomaly/early-warning detector, not a classifier. The GBM fusion was built and measured — it is degenerate at this sample size (83% positive class in 2 of 3 folds). The weighted rule is not a workaround; it is the correct design.
→ Unsupervised arms (VAE + zero-shot). Labels used only for EVALUATION, never training. Every performance claim cites n=1 or n=3, not a robust average.
CRITICAL
Arm B detects 1 of 5 events
The VAE detects large market dislocations. Four of the five anchors did not move the market enough to be anomalous. Abqaiq 0.95, Red Sea 0.67, Gulf of Oman 0.62, Fujairah 0.42 — all below the p95 threshold. The 38-day lead claim applies to one event only.
→ Never quote the 38-day figure without stating "on one event." Arm C exists precisely because Arm B fails on sharp incidents — the two arms are complementary by design and by measurement.
CRITICAL
Arm A (SAR/CNN) shows no reroute signal
Measured on 449 chips, orbit-matched, across the 2026 onset: Fujairah +0.0%, Khor Fakkan +0.0%. Mann-Whitney p > 0.6, effect sizes |r| ≤ 0.24. CNN test accuracy 0.837 — loses to a random forest on brightness statistics (0.946). At 15.6 m/px a vessel is 1–3 pixels; convolution has no structure to exploit.
→ Arm A weight = 0.00 in fusion. CFAR baseline retained. The "earliest physical confirmation" claim for Fujairah is unsupported. Never imply satellite confirms disruption from this arm's output.
CRITICAL
No live vessel positions for Gulf waters
aisstream.io has zero terrestrial receiver coverage in the Gulf (measured, not assumed). This is not fixable in code. Global Fishing Watch satellite AIS covers the Gulf but lags 4 days — usable for backtest, not live alerting. The vessel layer returns 501, not an empty map that could be mistaken for calm water.
→ /vessels returns 501 with full explanation. GFW documented as the backtest-only alternative. Satellite (Arm A) detects vessels regardless of AIS — the correct long-term response.
HIGH
Orbit direction is a 2× artifact at Khalifa
Same port, same water: Khalifa shows 51 vessels (ascending) vs 26 (descending) — a factor of 2 caused purely by satellite look direction, not congestion. Never compare passes of different orbit direction without per-port normalisation. The artifact is absent at the other three ports.
→ Orbit recorded on every chip. Per-orbit comparison enforced in all analysis. Never report a raw count change without specifying orbit.
HIGH
SAR sampling density correlates with the label
The 2026 Hormuz window has ~2× the revisit density of all three historical windows (S1C/S1D are new satellites). A model can learn "dense sampling → 2026 → crisis" without seeing any future value. TimeSeriesSplit cannot catch this.
→ SAR features aggregated to weekly cadence. Raw observation counts never exposed as features. Sampling density is a documented property of each window.
HIGH
Arm B is not Gulf-specific
The VAE fires on any large sustained market dislocation. COVID (median 4.74 vs 0.60 for Hormuz) and Ukraine (max spread 12.63) both trigger it. Geography comes from Arm C, not price. Never present the market arm alone as evidence of a Gulf event.
→ COVID excluded as a declared reasoned exclusion. Arm C (multilingual GDELT text) supplies the geography. Both arms required for a confident Gulf-specific signal.
